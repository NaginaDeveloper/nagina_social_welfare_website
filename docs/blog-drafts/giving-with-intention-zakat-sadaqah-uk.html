Zakat, Sadaqah, Lillah and Fitrana: What They Mean

People often ask what the difference is between the main types of giving, so here it is in plain words.

Zakat: the obligatory charity

Zakat is the obligatory charity that is one of the five pillars of Islam. It is paid on qualifying wealth above the applicable threshold, subject to the relevant conditions, and is commonly calculated at 2.5%. If you’re not sure what your own figure is, our Zakat calculator can help you estimate it from eligible assets such as gold, silver and savings. If you’re searching for a Zakat calculator in the UK or want to donate Zakat in the UK, review the information on our Zakat and donation pages. For anything particular to your situation, it’s always wise to respectfully consult a qualified scholar.

Sadaqah: voluntary giving

Sadaqah is voluntary giving, and it can be any amount at any time. A pound given with a sincere heart counts. If you are considering a Sadaqah donation in the UK, regular giving can be one way to support charitable work throughout the year.

Lillah: giving for the sake of Allah

Lillah means giving purely for the sake of Allah Ta’ala, and it is often used for the running of institutions such as madrasas and centres. Before making a Lillah donation in the UK, check the purpose of the fund and how your contribution will be used.

Fitrana: charity before Eid ul-Fitr

Fitrana is the charity given before Eid ul-Fitr at the end of Ramadan, so that everyone can celebrate the day with dignity. If you plan to make a Fitrana donation in the UK, check the applicable amount, timing and religious guidance for the relevant year.

If you’d like to give Zakat, Sadaqah, Lillah or Fitrana in the UK, visit our Donate page for the available options. Please check the purpose of each fund, particularly if you intend your contribution to count as Zakat.

Giving with Intention: Simple Tips for Donating Zakat and Sadaqah

You don’t need to be wealthy to make a difference. What matters most is the intention behind the gift and the habit of giving. Whether you are exploring Zakat, Sadaqah or Lillah donations, choose a charitable purpose that matches your intention and check the relevant guidance. A few things that help:

  • Give a little, often. A small amount every week or month does more than a big gift you forget about.
  • Work out your Zakat early. Don’t leave it to the last minute. Use the Zakat calculator on our website, then set the money aside. If you have complex financial circumstances, respectfully consult a qualified scholar for guidance.
  • Give to people you can trust. Check a charity’s registration. Nagina Social Welfare (UK) is listed under charity number 1196514; you can check the details on the Charity Commission register.
  • Involve your children. Let them put a coin in or choose who the gift goes to. It teaches them more than a lecture will.
